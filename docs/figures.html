The dominant axis

Property type decides almost everything

HM Land Registry's specification names five reasons a sale will not match: land without a postal address, garages sold separately, new builds absent from the Royal Mail address file, incomplete title addresses, and partial transactions of larger titles. Four of those five describe property that never had an addressable location to begin with — and that is exactly what the data shows.

The expected finding

The new-build flag, on its own, shows almost nothing

The intuitive story is that new builds go unmatched because the address file has not caught up with them. The specification says so explicitly. Put the flag on a chart by itself, though, and the two bars are nearly the same height.

The reveal

Hold property type constant and a sixfold effect appears

The house rows in Figure 2 are compressed against a scale that "Other" stretches to 44%. Drawn on their own scale, they separate sharply. This is the address-file lag the specification describes, and it was hidden by composition: "Other" makes up 6.7% of established sales but only 1.9% of new builds, dragging the established rate up, while flats are over-represented among new builds and unmatched at the same rate either way.

The effect cannot be stated as a single number. Cochran's Q on the log risk ratios is 332.6 on 4 degrees of freedom, p ≈ 1.0 × 10⁻⁷⁰. The strata are not estimating one common effect — they run from 0.78 to 6.54 and reverse direction between houses and land. A Mantel-Haenszel adjusted ratio of 1.45 can be computed, and is, but quoting it alone would average a sixfold effect in houses together with no effect in flats and a slightly protective one in land.

What this cannot tell you

  • One month only The look-up is forward-only and this is the first release in existence. Nothing here establishes a trend, and July is a month of registrations, not of sales.
  • Not the back catalogue Thirty years of historic Price Paid Data carry no UPRN at all. These rates describe newly published records, not the complete file.
  • Nothing to audit against HM Land Registry has published no official match rate. Every figure here comes from the repository's own extraction.
  • Unmatched ≠ unmatchable A missing UPRN means the matching process produced no link. It does not mean the property has no UPRN.
  • Grain runs both ways No sale maps to more than one UPRN this month, so a left join preserves the row count exactly. But 1,055 UPRNs carry 2 to 7 sales each — anything aggregated by property double-counts.
